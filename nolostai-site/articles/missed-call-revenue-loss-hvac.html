How Much Money Is Your HVAC Company Actually Losing to Missed Calls?

Written for people who run a truck, not a call center · Updated 2026

Bottom line: There is no single number that fits every shop. Your cost depends on how many calls come in, how many go unanswered, and what a typical job is worth to you. The worksheet below lets you plug in your own numbers.

Why you've never noticed this money is gone

Here's the thing nobody tells you about a missed call: it doesn't show up anywhere. It's not a bounced check or a bad review sitting in your inbox staring at you. The homeowner who called, got nothing, and hung up just... doesn't exist in your world. They never became a lead. They never became a no-show. They never became anything, because as far as your CRM and your dispatch board are concerned, that call never happened.

That's exactly why this leak is so easy to ignore. There's no line item that says "money lost to missed calls." It's just quieter revenue than you would've had, month after month, and you chalk it up to a slow season instead of what it actually is.

The math, no fluff

It's three numbers multiplied together: how many calls come in each week, what share go unanswered, and what a typical job is worth. Pull the first two from your phone records and the third from your own books.

Your numberWhere to find it
A. Calls per weekYour phone carrier or call log
B. Calls that go unansweredMissed and voicemail entries in the same log
C. Average value of a jobYour own invoices
Estimated weekly costB × C × the share of those callers who would have booked
Estimated yearly costWeekly cost × 52

Example only, not a benchmark: if 4 calls a week go unanswered, a typical job is $300, and half of those callers would have booked, that is 4 × $300 × 50% = $600 a week, or about $31,200 a year. Your numbers will be different, and that is the point. Be honest about the share who would have booked, and your answer will be more useful than any industry average.

Emergency no-heat and no-cool calls are often worth more than routine calls, and they tend to come when your crew is already stretched thin. Rough timing, right?

What actually stops this

You don't fix this by working harder or hiring somebody to babysit a phone 24 hours a day — nobody's doing that, and it wouldn't be cheap if they did. How do I figure out what a missed call costs me?

It depends on your jobs. Use your own average job value and the share of callers who would have booked, as in the worksheet above.

How many calls is my shop actually missing?

Check your phone carrier's call log for missed and voicemail entries over a few typical weeks. Busy season is worth checking separately.

How do I figure out my own number?

Take your weekly call volume, multiply it by your miss rate, multiply that by your average job value, then multiply by 52. That's your real annual number.
