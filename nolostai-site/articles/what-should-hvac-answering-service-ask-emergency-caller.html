What Should an HVAC Call Agent Actually Ask an Emergency Caller?

Written for people who run a truck, not a call center · Updated 2026

Bottom line: Figure out the system, the actual symptom, how long it's been happening, and whether anyone in the house is medically vulnerable to the temperature — and ask about a gas smell first, because that changes everything about how the call gets handled.

The questions that actually matter, in the order they matter

  • What system are we talking about? Furnace, AC, heat pump, water heater
  • What's actually happening? No heat, no cool, weird noise, gas smell, water everywhere, total shutdown
  • How long's this been going on? Just started, or they've been dealing with it for days
  • Is anyone in the house especially vulnerable to the temperature? Elderly folks, a baby, someone with a health condition
  • Do you smell gas? — this one jumps the line. If yes, that's an evacuate-and-call-911-or-the-gas-company situation, not a scheduling conversation

Why the order actually matters

If you ask about safety first, a real emergency — no heat during a freeze with an elderly person in the house, or a gas smell — gets flagged and moved immediately, instead of sitting behind a bunch of routine questions about maintenance plans nobody cares about in that moment.

What happens once you know what you're dealing with

  1. Quote the flat diagnostic fee so there's no surprise later
  2. Real emergencies go straight to whoever's on call — not the regular scheduling queue
  3. Routine stuff gets booked to the next open slot
  4. Confirm everything back to the caller before hanging up
