Is an AI Answering Service Worth It for a One-Truck HVAC Technician?

Written for people who run a truck, not a call center · Last reviewed October 2026

Bottom line: Only your own numbers can answer that, and the math is simple. Count the calls you miss in a typical week, work out what an average job earns you in profit, and compare that to the monthly cost. If one booked job covers the cost, the tool has a path to paying for itself. We won't hand you a made-up average, because every shop is different, and No Lost AI is only as good as GoHighLevel (GHL), the platform it's built on.

Why a solo tech asks this question

When you’re the only truck, you can’t answer the phone while you’re under a unit or up in an attic. A call that goes to voicemail may or may not call back, and that missed call is a customer who may have phoned the next company on the list. The question isn’t whether an answering service is nice to have. It’s whether it earns back what it costs.

A five-step worksheet

Grab a pen and use your own real numbers.

  1. Count missed calls for one week. Check your phone’s call log. Count calls that went unanswered and weren’t from people you already know.
  2. Find your average job profit. Not revenue. What you keep after parts, fuel, and your time.
  3. Estimate your close rate. Out of the people you do speak to, about how many become booked jobs?
  4. Write down the monthly cost. Use the current price on the site, not a number you heard secondhand.
  5. Calculate your breakeven. Monthly cost ÷ average job profit = the number of extra jobs you’d need each month for it to pay for itself.

If that breakeven number is smaller than what your missed calls and close rate suggest you’re leaving behind, the math works in your favor. If it isn’t, it may not be the right fit yet, and it’s better to know that now.

What the worksheet doesn’t capture

Some value doesn’t show up in a spreadsheet: not stopping a job to answer, getting your evenings back, or customers who get a response instead of a voicemail. Some costs don’t show up either, such as the time to review how the agent handles your calls. Weigh both honestly.

How do I know if an AI answering service will pay for itself?

Count your missed calls for a week, find your average job profit and close rate, then divide the monthly cost by the profit per job. That gives you the number of extra jobs you need each month to break even.

Should I use revenue or profit in the math?

Use profit. It's what you keep after parts, fuel, and your time, so it gives you an honest breakeven.

What if I only miss a few calls a week?
